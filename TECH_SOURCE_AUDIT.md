# Development card source audit

The campaign starts in 49 BCE. A card represents learning, local adaptation, or organization, not the first invention of its subject. Game costs and effects are balance values, not measurements from the sources. The drawings are original interpretive diagrams, not excavated objects.

| Card IDs | Evidence now linked from the game | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `roadmaking`, `road-milestones`, `bridge-engineering` | [UNESCO, Via Appia](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1708): road begun in 312 BCE; the property includes bridges and milestones. | The heritage property includes additions through late antiquity. A surviving bridge or marker is not necessarily dated to 49 BCE. |
| `barracks-design` | [Numantia archaeological site](https://numanciasoria.es/en/divulgacion/the-archaeological-hall-of-garray): reconstruction of Scipio's siege camps and a legionary barrack hut. | A museum reconstruction supports camp planning, not a universal Republican barrack design. |
| `stone-fortification` | [Musée d'Archéologie nationale, Alesia](https://musee-archeologienationale.fr/en/node/1592): Caesar's double field fortification and camps, 52 BCE. | The historical note and displayed name now say *fortification planning*; the stable internal ID is retained for old saves. The source does not establish that all such works were stone. |
| `quarrying` | [Oxford Roman Economy Project, quarries](https://oxrep.web.ox.ac.uk/stone-quarries-database): Roman-period quarry gazetteer. | Its authors warn that precise dates and coordinates are often uncertain. |
| `metal-extraction` | [Oxford Roman Economy Project, mines](https://oxrep.web.ox.ac.uk/mines-database): recorded Roman mines and extraction methods. | Its authors warn that dating most individual mines is difficult. |
| `siege-machinery` | [Caesar, *Civil Wars* II.1–22](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Caesar/Civil_Wars/2A%2A.html): earthworks, covered galleries, and towers at Massilia in 49 BCE. | Caesar's account is a participant's text; the card does not imply that every siege had these works. |
| `library-organization` | [Dix and Houston, *Mélanges de l'École française de Rome*](https://www.persee.fr/doc/mefr_0223-5102_2006_num_118_2_10261): private Republican collections precede Roman public libraries. | The 49 BCE card and building now refer to a private collection. Stable IDs are retained for save compatibility. |
| `aqueduct-refinement` | [Sovrintendenza Capitolina, Aqua Marcia](https://www.sovraintendenzaroma.it/i_luoghi/roma_antica/monumenti/acquedotto_marcio): built in 144 BCE, with major restorations later. | The card describes distribution improvements; it does not claim that aqueducts were invented in 49 BCE or that later restorations had already happened. |
| `urban-drainage`, `sewer-improvement` | [Sovrintendenza Capitolina, Cloaca Maxima](https://www.sovraintendenzaroma.it/i_luoghi/roma_antica/monumenti/cloaca_massima): an archaic drainage channel altered repeatedly in the Republican and Imperial periods. | The surviving monument includes later fabric. The cards now describe improvement of older works and avoid modern hygiene claims. |
| `bath-construction` | [Pompeii Archaeological Park, Stabian and Republican Baths](https://pompeiisites.org/en/excavations-plan-en/stabian-baths-and-republican-baths/): both were built in the second century BCE, with later changes to water and heating systems. | The card describes an existing Republican practice; the surviving baths are not presented as unchanged 49 BCE structures. |
| `tax-farming` | [Cicero, *In Verrem* 2.3.13–15, 20–22, 41–43](https://www.attalus.org/cicero/verres23.html): Sicilian tithe rights were auctioned; Cicero alleged that Verres's collection rules damaged farmers. | A prosecution speech from 70 BCE is adversarial evidence about Sicily. The treasury and loyalty values are game balance, not quantitative findings. |
| `estate-organization` | [Cato, *On Agriculture* 1–2](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cato/De_Agricultura/A%2A.html): farm purchase, oversight of labor and yields, and accounts. | Cato's second-century BCE text is prescriptive for an owner; it does not prove that every estate used these practices or that they increased food by a fixed amount. |
| `rhetorical-education` | [Cicero, *De Oratore* 1.155](https://www.attalus.org/cicero/deoratore1B.html): translation of Greek speeches as an oratorical exercise. | Written in 55 BCE, the dialogue gives an elite educational ideal, not a measured research discount or evidence of universal schooling. |
| `grain-storage` | [Cato, *On Agriculture* 92](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cato/De_Agricultura/F%2A.html): advice for protecting granary grain against pests. | This is a second-century BCE recommendation, not proof that the recipe worked or that every granary used it. |
| `supply-organization` | [Salido Domínguez, *Germania* 93 (2015)](https://journals.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/index.php/germania/article/download/33997/27684): excavated Republican military granaries in Hispania; military grain storage varied by campaign. | Finds are geographically concentrated and do not establish a universal standardized supply system in 49 BCE. Equipment storage and gameplay bonuses are abstractions. |
| `grain-processing` | [Cato, *On Agriculture* 11](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cato/De_Agricultura/A%2A.html): donkey-driven and hand mills listed as estate equipment. | An equipment list does not quantify output or prove identical milling practice across the Mediterranean. |

The remaining cards still need a direct source review. A broad source link should be treated as background, especially where the evidence is Imperial rather than Republican. Before changing another card, check the date, location, and the exact claim in both languages.
