# Map Lab · data, licensing and limitations

The main campaign map's `../assets/mediterranean-relief-main.png` is generated by `../tools/build-main-relief.mjs` from 368 of the same bundled Mapzen/AWS Terrarium zoom-7 elevation tiles credited below. Its 2,400 × 1,300 pixels are projected into the main SVG's 1,200 × 650 equirectangular frame, clipped to the separately sourced GSHHG land shape, and used only for physical shading. Political mode draws this relief at 48% opacity to keep control colors and operational borders legible; terrain mode shows its full strength. It is modern elevation, not a reconstruction of ancient terrain. The 121 controllable cells remain gameplay partitions.

At settled close zoom, the atlas automatically shades the bundled modern DEM for a clearer landform view, with warmer shadows around Italy. The Italian close hillshade now uses 0.14 exaggeration after matched Ariminum and Apennine screenshots showed that the former 0.23 made ridges appear too crumpled; coastline and source elevation are unchanged. Shading pauses while the map moves and can be turned off in Settings; a saved manual choice takes precedence. It adds no historical elevation claim or new external data.

The atlas and campaign can transfer their current state in the same browser tab and share compatible save slots. The default grand campaign has 121 controllable strategic territories, including 30 in Italy and Sicily. Its settlement anchors were manually curated as approximate ancient-site positions. Territory boundaries are **gameplay partitions**, not a surveyed 49 BCE province map. The polygons were generated by clipping nearest-anchor cells to the adapted AWMC/Cliopatria historical outlines. Twelve sites outside those source outlines use small land-clipped catchments with explicit uncertainty. The physical coast and modern elevation are a visualization, not evidence for 49 BCE vegetation or settlement. Cyrene's anchor follows the [UNESCO World Heritage site coordinates](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/190/); those coordinates locate the ancient city, not a precise civil-war-era political boundary. Disconnected source outlines are assigned only to nearby sites, preventing Leptis Magna from acquiring the Cyrenaica lobe.

The bilingual sea and landform names in `physical-labels.js` are original orientation labels placed over the modern physical map. Their anchor coordinates are cartographic layout choices, not surveyed ancient boundaries or archaeological claims.

The small close-scale settlement labels in `ancient-place-labels.js` are non-interactive gazetteer references, separate from controllable campaign cities. [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/) identifies [Caesena](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/393397) and [Pisaurum](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/413256); place content is CC BY 3.0, credited to Pleiades contributors. Their approximate coordinates follow the Pleiades-derived [ISAW Pliny the Elder's World dataset](https://isaw.nyu.edu/research/pliny-the-elder). These labels do not assert exact settlement footprints, ancient shorelines, or 49 BCE political control. Nearby Fanum Fortunae is omitted because its label collides with Pisaurum at the atlas's current maximum scale. Sena Gallica is omitted because Pleiades records its destruction in 82 BCE and a legible settlement at the 49 BCE campaign start was not established for this layer.

At local zoom, land color and shaded relief come from the bundled modern elevation data. Earlier decorative stipple and wave patterns were removed after they made the sea and Nile Delta look grainy. They are not present in the current map.

  The Po mouth is a particularly visible anachronism. A [University of Bologna historical-geography study](https://amsacta.unibo.it/2781/1/escursionedeltadelpo.pdf) maps distinct Roman and later coastlines and dates the prominent northern delta peninsula to the 16th–17th centuries onward. An [Italian geological survey review](https://www.isprambiente.gov.it/files/pubblicazioni/periodicitecnici/memorie/memorielxiii/b24.pdf) also describes the modern delta lobe as a result of early 17th-century river diversion. The atlas retains the modern GSHHG shoreline, using a tapered 0.8 km smoothing pass on the long mainland ring within 12.30–12.85°E, 44.68–45.13°N before the existing two corner-rounding passes. Seven small island rings receive the existing corner-rounding pass but not the new Gaussian pass. The latter moved 115 source vertices by at most 0.552 km; within that local window, about 6.6 km² of former land becomes sea and 5.3 km² of former sea becomes land. Land and ocean are rebuilt from the same geometry; 121 settlement anchors and the land/sea route audits retain their prior classification. This is display-scale generalization of a modern source, not a reconstructed Roman shore. Its precise islands, river mouths and coastline must not be read as the 49 BCE landscape.

  The Nile Delta coast receives the same two corner-rounding passes only within 29.0–33.5°E, 30.5–32.2°N. The rebuilt land and ocean remain complementary; a 0.02° sample of 19,125 points found no gaps or overlaps. Matched close-view screenshots show fewer hard angles along the modern coastal lagoons. This is a visual generalization of GSHHG, not a reconstruction of the 49 BCE Nile mouths or delta shoreline. The river branches are the separate, coarser Natural Earth references credited below.

## Named road reconstructions

The displayed linework is a subset of [Ancient World Mapping Center road data](https://github.com/AWMC/geodata/tree/master/Cultural-Data/roads), derived from the Barrington Atlas and AWMC modifications to OpenStreetMap, © AWMC and OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). The derived database is `../republican-roads.js`, generated from AWMC `roads.geojson` blob `e5b53dfb7aab621eb25d31d66457562240524ff2` by `../tools/build-republican-roads.mjs`. It selects named features 637 (Via Appia, Rome–Capua), 1467 (Via Aemilia), and 1480 and 1550 (Via Flaminia), preserves the source feature IDs and source-map attributes, and rounds coordinates to five decimal places. Decimal precision is not a claim of survey accuracy.

The [Umbrian heritage authority](https://sabapumbria.cultura.gov.it/archeologia-e-territorio/archeologia-via-flaminia/) dates Via Flaminia's construction to 220–219 BCE. [LacusCurtius' reproduction of the Britannica entry](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Periods/Roman/Topics/Engineering/roads/Aemilia/Britannica_1911%2A.html) dates Via Aemilia to 187 BCE. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1708) dates the original Rome–Capua Via Appia to 312 BCE. These dates establish the historical roads, not the exact date of every mapped segment: the AWMC lines are later reconstructions, may incorporate changes or uncertain alignments, and are not presented as a surveyed 49 BCE network. Other unnamed or potentially later roads have not been imported. The lines are informational and do not change the campaign's abstract movement graph.

## Faction identity art direction

All faction marks, banners and counters are original vector drawings used as modern game identifiers; their colors, compositions, and use as faction flags are not claimed as historically attested. Caesar and the Senate are opposing Roman commands, not separate ancient states. Roman stacked discs evoke military standards and the Senate mark evokes the fasces of Republican magistrates. Roman standards and unit shield devices are discussed by the Metropolitan Museum: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-decoration-of-european-armor .

The Egyptian cornucopia is inspired by Ptolemaic coin types: https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Pergamon_and_the_Hellenistic_Kingdoms_of_the_Ancient_World.pdf . The Parthian bow evokes Arsacid archer coin types: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-parthian-empire-247-b-c-224-a-d . The Armenian tiara is inspired by the contemporary Artavasdes II coinage documented by the Central Bank of Armenia: https://www.cba.am/en/history-ancient-to-1918 . The remaining spear, palm, and unmarked bands are deliberately general gameplay identifiers, not attributed to any specific historical standard. Mixed-control Roman areas use a neutral shared treatment rather than implying a third sovereign faction.

### Egypt: joint rule and official reports
The 49 BCE opening recognizes Cleopatra VII and Ptolemy XIII as joint rulers, based on the Metropolitan Museum of Art chronological table: https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Pergamon_and_the_Hellenistic_Kingdoms_of_the_Ancient_World.pdf. In-game correspondence and subsequent succession are simulated, not historical evidence; official claims may be unverified or outdated.


### Galatian and Bosporan courts

Deiotarus’ western Galatian rule and relationship to Rome are summarized by the [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548724362); Pharnaces II’s Bosporan kingship is documented by the [Royal Library of Belgium’s numismatic catalog](https://silver.kbr.be/Pharnaces_II_of_Pontus_%28king_of_the_Bosporan_Kingdom%2C_63-47_BC%29). Pleiades describes [Gordion](https://pleiades.stoa.org/indexes-old/settlement/hellenistic-republican?b_start%3Aint=1900) and [Panticapaeum](https://pleiades.stoa.org/indexes-old/index-by-select-time-periods/roman?b_start%3Aint=15860); Pleiades content is CC BY 3.0, credited to its contributors. Approximate theater markers are game aids and do not claim that Gordion was Deiotarus’ capital or define political borders. The broad source polygons are not evidence of their exact internal limits. Routes, resources, forces, and later events are simulation abstractions.
