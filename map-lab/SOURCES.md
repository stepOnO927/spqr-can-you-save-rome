# Map Lab · data, licensing and limitations

The atlas and campaign can transfer their current state in the same browser tab and share compatible save slots. The default grand campaign has 120 controllable strategic territories, including 30 in Italy and Sicily. Its settlement anchors were manually curated as approximate ancient-site positions. Territory boundaries are **gameplay partitions**, not a surveyed 49 BCE province map. The polygons were generated by clipping nearest-anchor cells to the adapted AWMC/Cliopatria historical outlines. Eleven sites outside those source outlines use small land-clipped catchments with explicit uncertainty. The physical coast and modern elevation are a visualization, not evidence for 49 BCE vegetation or settlement.

- MapLibre GL JS 5.9.0, BSD 3-Clause: https://github.com/maplibre/maplibre-gl-js/blob/main/LICENSE.txt . Its JavaScript, CSS, and license are bundled in `vendor/` so the atlas map engine works offline.
- Natural Earth 1:10m land, ocean, and bathymetry vectors, public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/10m-bathymetry/ . The land and ocean layers share one high-resolution coastline. `../mediterranean-land.js`, `../mediterranean-ocean.js`, and `../mediterranean-bathymetry.js` contain Mediterranean crops generated from the [Natural Earth vector repository](https://github.com/nvkelso/natural-earth-vector) by `../tools/build-physical-map.mjs`. The four bathymetry bands represent approximate modern 200 m, 1,000 m, 2,000 m, and 3,000 m depth contours. The sea mask sits above the land elevation layer, so underwater terrain is never drawn as land. These vectors are bundled; coastal and sea-depth shapes remain available without map tile access.
- Natural Earth II idealized land cover, public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-raster-data/10m-natural-earth-2/ and https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/ . The medium `NE2_LR_LC.tif` raster was reprojected to 362 bundled Web Mercator tiles in `landcover/` with `../tools/build-idealized-landcover.mjs`; the original TIFF is not required at runtime. Natural Earth describes this raster as a general-purpose, low-human-influence portrayal suitable for historical maps. It is an illustrative environment, **not evidence of vegetation or land use in 49 BCE**. The overlay fades out by close zoom so it does not become an enlarged blur, and the vector ocean mask covers its white water background.
- The zoomed Italy locator, `italy-locator.svg`, is regenerated from the same Natural Earth land crop by `../tools/build-italy-locator.mjs`. It shows a modern coast and the current map center for orientation; it does not mark a 49 BCE political boundary or an ancient shoreline.
- AWS / Mapzen Terrain Tiles, Terrarium digital elevation model and contributors, sources and attribution: https://github.com/tilezen/joerd/blob/master/docs/attribution.md and https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/ . The 1,173 bundled tiles in `terrain/` cover the playable Mediterranean at zooms 4–6 and Italy and the Adriatic at zooms 7–9, the highest zoom used by the atlas. `../tools/fetch-atlas-elevation.ps1` and `../tools/fetch-atlas-elevation-z9.mjs` record their source coordinates. The underlying elevation combines regional sources, including Copernicus EU-DEM, NOAA ETOPO1, and USGS SRTM/GMTED2010; see the linked provider attribution for the full list. MapLibre color relief shades the land from modern elevation values; hillshade adds directional terrain detail. Terrain is present-day elevation, not an ancient reconstruction.
- Natural Earth 1:10m rivers, lake centerlines and natural lake polygons, public domain: https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/10m-physical-vectors/ . The bundled `../mediterranean-rivers.js` and `../mediterranean-lakes.js` were derived from Natural Earth vector revision `ca96624a56bd078437bca8184e78163e5039ad19` by `../tools/build-physical-hydrography.mjs`. The build excludes modern canals and reservoirs, retains river scale rank for map styling, and rounds coordinates to five decimal places. These are modern generalized physical references, not surveyed 49 BCE river courses or proof of an ancient lake boundary. The older 1:110m `../rivers.js` remains in the repository for provenance but is no longer used by the atlas.
- Political shapes are the existing adapted AWMC c. 60 BCE (ODbL 1.0) and Seshat Cliopatria v0.2.1 (CC BY 4.0) data, with source details and transformations in ../MAP_SOURCES.md. A shared polygon does not establish separate internal boundaries or an exact allegiance at every point.
- Grand campaign polygons: `../grand-territory-polygons.js`, regenerated by `node tools/build-grand-polygons.mjs`. Settlement and route data are in `../grand-map-data.js`; route connections and economic values are playable approximations, not claims of excavated roads or audited ancient revenues. The historical political outlines remain in `../historical-territories.js`. The older 1:110m land dataset is retained in `../land.js` for data provenance.
- Place identifications: Pleiades ancient place gazetteer https://pleiades.stoa.org/ ; Salona and civil-war context: https://www.armus.hr/en/collections/roman-provincial-collection ; Caesar and Illyricum: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/illyricum-in-roman-politics-229-bcad-68/construction-of-illyricum-caesar-in-illyricum-and-the-civil-wars-5944-bc/8C06B68D9FE789689625316A7B7A4E5B .
- `../tools/audit-site-coast.mjs` compares all 120 settlement anchors to the bundled modern Natural Earth land geometry. Two port markers, Iol and Iader, lie about 0.6 km and 0.3 km beyond that polygon's shoreline. The script flags any anchor farther than 2 km offshore for manual review. This is a cartographic screen, not evidence of a surveyed ancient shoreline or a reason to move a historically supported port point inland.
- Fonts: Cormorant Garamond and Manrope, SIL Open Font License 1.1, served by Google Fonts. All UI, markers and banners in this prototype are original CSS/SVG-like geometric designs.

All terrain levels used by the playable atlas are bundled; web fonts may require a network connection. The land/sea shapes and colors remain visible offline. No ancient vegetation layer, complete dated province subdivision, exact 49 BCE road alignment, or exact 49 BCE control survey is claimed.

The restrained land palette uses modern elevation color. Directional hill shading is off by default, with stronger local relief at close Italy zoom when switched on with REL. The setting is remembered. The eleven sea zones, initial fleet strength, ship costs, commercial lanes, cargo quantities and piracy pressures in `../naval.js` are gameplay abstractions, not measured 49 BCE records. They do not imply exact ancient maritime borders or shipping volumes.

## Named road reconstructions

The displayed linework is a subset of [Ancient World Mapping Center road data](https://github.com/AWMC/geodata/tree/master/Cultural-Data/roads), derived from the Barrington Atlas and AWMC modifications to OpenStreetMap, © AWMC and OpenStreetMap contributors, available under the [Open Database License 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/). The derived database is `../republican-roads.js`, generated from AWMC `roads.geojson` blob `e5b53dfb7aab621eb25d31d66457562240524ff2` by `../tools/build-republican-roads.mjs`. It selects named features 637 (Via Appia, Rome–Capua), 1467 (Via Aemilia), and 1480 and 1550 (Via Flaminia), preserves the source feature IDs and source-map attributes, and rounds coordinates to five decimal places. Decimal precision is not a claim of survey accuracy.

The [Umbrian heritage authority](https://sabapumbria.cultura.gov.it/archeologia-e-territorio/archeologia-via-flaminia/) dates Via Flaminia's construction to 220–219 BCE. [LacusCurtius' reproduction of the Britannica entry](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Gazetteer/Periods/Roman/Topics/Engineering/roads/Aemilia/Britannica_1911%2A.html) dates Via Aemilia to 187 BCE. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1708) dates the original Rome–Capua Via Appia to 312 BCE. These dates establish the historical roads, not the exact date of every mapped segment: the AWMC lines are later reconstructions, may incorporate changes or uncertain alignments, and are not presented as a surveyed 49 BCE network. Other unnamed or potentially later roads have not been imported. The lines are informational and do not change the campaign's abstract movement graph.

## Faction identity art direction

All faction marks, banners and counters are original vector drawings used as modern game identifiers; their colors, compositions, and use as faction flags are not claimed as historically attested. Caesar and the Senate are opposing Roman commands, not separate ancient states. Roman stacked discs evoke military standards and the Senate mark evokes the fasces of Republican magistrates. Roman standards and unit shield devices are discussed by the Metropolitan Museum: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-decoration-of-european-armor .

The Egyptian cornucopia is inspired by Ptolemaic coin types: https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Pergamon_and_the_Hellenistic_Kingdoms_of_the_Ancient_World.pdf . The Parthian bow evokes Arsacid archer coin types: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/the-parthian-empire-247-b-c-224-a-d . The Armenian tiara is inspired by the contemporary Artavasdes II coinage documented by the Central Bank of Armenia: https://www.cba.am/en/history-ancient-to-1918 . The remaining spear, palm, and unmarked bands are deliberately general gameplay identifiers, not attributed to any specific historical standard. Mixed-control Roman areas use a neutral shared treatment rather than implying a third sovereign faction.

### Egypt: joint rule and official reports
The 49 BCE opening recognizes Cleopatra VII and Ptolemy XIII as joint rulers, based on the Metropolitan Museum of Art chronological table: https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Pergamon_and_the_Hellenistic_Kingdoms_of_the_Ancient_World.pdf. In-game correspondence and subsequent succession are simulated, not historical evidence; official claims may be unverified or outdated.


### Galatian and Bosporan courts

Deiotarus’ western Galatian rule and relationship to Rome are summarized by the [Oxford Classical Dictionary](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548724362); Pharnaces II’s Bosporan kingship is documented by the [Royal Library of Belgium’s numismatic catalog](https://silver.kbr.be/Pharnaces_II_of_Pontus_%28king_of_the_Bosporan_Kingdom%2C_63-47_BC%29). Pleiades describes [Gordion](https://pleiades.stoa.org/indexes-old/settlement/hellenistic-republican?b_start%3Aint=1900) and [Panticapaeum](https://pleiades.stoa.org/indexes-old/index-by-select-time-periods/roman?b_start%3Aint=15860); Pleiades content is CC BY 3.0, credited to its contributors. Approximate theater markers are game aids and do not claim that Gordion was Deiotarus’ capital or define political borders. The broad source polygons are not evidence of their exact internal limits. Routes, resources, forces, and later events are simulation abstractions.
