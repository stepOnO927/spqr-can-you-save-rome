# World expansion progress

This file tracks the four supplied specifications as one development goal. The expansion is **in progress**. A functioning slice is not a claim of world coverage.

## Implemented in this checkpoint

- A deliberately fictional village in the Ravenna hinterland now demonstrates the local settlement layer without presenting invented coordinates as historical fact. Its saved record contains population, council, grain specialty, finite stored food, production, civilian need, satisfaction, security and improvement projects. Granary and irrigation construction take money, food and months; nonplayer Caesar can use the same construction rules when funds permit. Normal purchases reserve two months of civilian food; emergency requisition damages local satisfaction, security and theater loyalty. An overloaded friendly army may buy limited village support, drawing from the same finite store. A conquered village follows its host theater while retaining its prior legal title and losing any unfinished project. Map Lab exposes the village and actions; the Inspector checks its record. This is one representative village, not yet regional coverage.
- Market, well, road and wall projects now add concrete effects to that village. The market raises local revenue and permits cheaper, larger civilian-safe purchases; the well improves satisfaction and conditional population growth; the road raises local market access and the host theater's real supply capacity by one; the wall raises security and a small local militia while reducing occupation shock. Civilian food need rises with population; growth pauses without a production surplus. All projects have bounded levels, upfront resources and construction time. Existing saves containing the village gain only missing project fields, preserving population, food and ownership.
- Fields and a community shrine complete the village's initial eight-project development menu. Fields raise monthly food production; the shrine improves satisfaction at completion and during well supplied quarters. A village with at least 700 people, a granary and viable food production may qualify as a market town through a market, road, access and local satisfaction, or a fortified settlement through a wall, road and security. Market-town status adds real local revenue and one more civilian-safe food unit per purchase; a fortified settlement suffers a smaller occupation shock. These are derived local statuses that can recede; they do not create new sovereign states or change legal title.

- Reproducible audit of Roman and Han territory references, routes, historical polygons and local entity placement (`node scripts/audit-territories.mjs`). The audit reports 24 Roman theaters, 28 routes, 15 historical polygon features, 94 Han regions and 124 Han routes. It does not equate map imagery with ownerless land.
- Canonical theater record is still `state.regions`. It now keeps controller, legal sovereign, military occupier, administrative status and control date. Conquest, siege, secession and command reunification use one transfer function. Legacy saves gain default fields without changing owners or foreign reports.
- Occupied theaters yield less tax. A player or AI with a garrison, resources and adequate loyalty can fund a three-month administration project; loss of the garrison pauses it. Administration improves tax collection without changing legal sovereignty.
- Main campaign and Map Lab show the new political layers for owned theaters while concealing foreign actual state. Non-playable geography can be inspected without claiming it is vacant. A city marker remains clickable when an army is on top of it.
- Massilia is a sourced, autonomous city-state within the existing Narbonensian theater. It has its own controller, legal title, militia, resources, delayed and fallible port-access/protectorate offers, tribute, siege, occupation and local administration. The host theater does not change owner when the city is taken. Its military and resource figures are explicitly simulated. See [Caesar, *Civil Wars* I.34–36](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Caesar/Civil_Wars/1B%2A.html) for the historical city and conflict.
- AI can negotiate port access, besiege after a refused offer and administer captured territory through the same functions. The Inspector validates local entity geography, type, controller and militia.
- Border policies now persist in new and upgraded saves. Military transit checks use reported control in route previews and actual control on arrival. Trade and ordinary alliances confer no automatic army passage. Military access treaties may be bilateral or limited to a named permitted party and reported theater; the government panel can propose either scope. Existing full military-access treaties retain their behavior.
- Diplomatic agreements and ordinary alliances now travel as dated courier proposals in both campaign and Map Lab. Sending spends 2 treasury, the recipient may refuse or the letter may be lost, and treaty costs and effects occur only after an accepted reply. A limited, one-way military-access scope stays attached to the letter and is checked again on arrival; it cannot become an unrestricted grant during delivery. The main government panel exposes the proposal and the Imperial Mailbox exposes letters.
- Roman courts may now propose a protectorate or client-kingdom relationship through the existing four-month agreement courier. Both are public, dated, expiring treaties: the subject keeps its theaters, armies, officials and treasury, while the suzerain owes defense. A protectorate retains independent diplomacy, full domestic autonomy and no automatic access or tribute. A client kingdom retains broad internal autonomy but has limited external war and treaty authority, pays two treasury each quarter only above a protected reserve, and grants one-way suzerain military and commercial passage. Its political loyalty and independence aspiration change with tribute performance and can lead to renunciation. A suzerain at war may request a bounded contingent from a neighboring client; 400 manpower and three food leave the subject's real reserves, travel along a mapped border with terrain delay, and reach the suzerain only on arrival. A closed or lost route blocks or loses the shipment. Multiple suzerains and circular dependencies are rejected; breach or war removes treaty rights. A proposed protector must have enough real military capacity to defend the subject, evaluated only when the recipient considers the letter. Hard and expert AI may send a client proposal to one reported nonplayer neighbor through the same courier, without immediately binding either party. The Map Lab has an own-relationship suzerainty mode based on reported theater control. Puppet regimes, Han princely institutions and wider foreign subject intelligence remain future work.
- Map Lab now has a military-access mode based on the player's reported theater controllers and its own concluded rights. It separates own control, full and limited military passage, wartime access, commercial passage without army access, closed army borders, unknown controllers and mixed historical polygons. The expandable theater list shows the date of each control report and opens the existing theater panel; the mode does not infer actual foreign control from game state. Physical routes and arrival checks remain governed by the existing movement and border rules.
- Local polities now have a separate, per-faction discovery record with unknown, geographic, contacted, identified, surveyed and documented levels. A new Roman campaign identifies its own local places, directly adjacent non-village communities and historically public Roman towns and Massilia, while a nearby village can remain only geographically known. A distant small kingdom stays present and simulated but is not named on an uninformed player's Map Lab. A successful traveler control report identifies local communities only after the return message arrives; army arrival surveys them. Discovery is monotonic and survives save upgrades, while the Inspector flags invalid records. This is the initial local-entity discovery layer; the broader world still needs more settlements, additional discovery methods and a dedicated exploration map.
- Map Lab now offers an explicit unauthorized border crossing for a known closed neighbor. On arrival, a real defending army may repel the crossing with limited losses; an undefended frontier records an unopposed crossing. Either outcome creates a persistent local incident, harms relations and credibility, and does not automatically declare war or transfer the theater. The player log reveals own losses without giving exact foreign casualties.
- A player involved in an incident can resolve it from a Map Lab panel. The crossing side can withdraw, pay compensation and withdraw, or seek one-way local passage. The defending side can protest, tolerate, or explicitly escalate through the normal war declaration system. The Inspector checks incident identities, geography and losses; the panel does not reveal exact foreign casualties.
- AI defenders protest an actual crossing after observation, while AI attackers respond to a protest with compensation or withdrawal according to resources and relations. The player is not auto-committed to a response. Both sides use the same incident-resolution functions.
- Map Lab now compares up to three long-distance paths on the existing theater graph. The planner uses only the faction's dated control reports, labels treaty passage, missing permission, sea cost, uncertain control and supply risk, and lets the player inspect the next adjacent step. It never declares an unknown route legally open or creates map geography. March orders still validate each step and recheck actual control on arrival.
- A controlled or access-granted Massilia can host a forward foothold. Its level-one camp needs money, food, a local garrison and a safe connection; higher levels require city and host-theater control, stronger garrisons, time and eventually local administration. Construction pauses if the safe route or garrison is lost. Food stock is transferred from faction reserves into a capped local store and consumed by a local army's rest; an isolated base does not grant a global range bonus. Loss of local rights records the former base before removing it.
- The Italian slice now has Ariminum, Capua and Brundisium as separate Roman towns within existing theaters, grounded in [Caesar, *Civil Wars* I.1–29](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Caesar/Civil_Wars/1A%2A.html). Their opening local controller and legal sovereign are the Senate; troop and resource figures are gameplay estimates. A city can submit after its host theater is taken and a real garrison is present, or fall after an army-backed siege. Either result leaves the theater and local legal title distinct. AI uses the same delayed submission and siege path and can fund a connected foothold.
- Peace letters can now include one explicit theater or local-town title-recognition term for a place the sender already controls and the recipient legally holds. The term is recorded with the letter, can be refused or lost, is rechecked when a reply arrives, and changes only the specified legal title after valid acceptance. A theater and its towns remain separate. The Map Lab shows the choice and the incoming/outgoing term; unmodified peace offers still preserve current titles.
- Occupied local towns and Massilia now track simulated resistance. A secure garrison and connected route reduce it; lost security, a cut route and high taxes increase it. Hostile Italian towns reduce the theater owner's monthly revenue, while controlled towns contribute only when resistance is below 60. A town at peak resistance can revert to its former legal authority only without a real garrison; the host theater stays with its controller, and an invalidated foothold is removed. The Map Lab shows the player's own local security and resistance, and the Inspector rejects impossible resistance values.
- Bibracte is now a sourced Aeduan local polity inside the existing Gaul theater, based on [Caesar, *Gallic War* VII.55–62](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Caesar/Gallic_War/7E%2A.html). Its point on Mont Beuvray is approximate and does not claim a tribal border; troop and resource figures are gameplay estimates. It retains local council control at the opening and can negotiate access or a protectorate, be besieged, and host a funded camp with finite food. Taking or accessing the town does not transfer the Gaul theater.
- Foothold food now moves in paid, dated shipments over an actual access-checked theater route. Travel time increases with land distance, sea legs and difficult terrain. Dispatched food is removed from faction reserves, counts against depot capacity while in transit, arrives only after the route remains open for the stated time, and can be lost after three blocked months. Player and AI use the same shipment action. Saved shipment records retain their route and remaining time; the Map Lab shows the player's pending cargo and transport quote. These shipments feed the separate Roman field-army supply assessment described below.
- Roman field armies now have an expedition supply assessment tied to integrated home theaters, access-checked routes, local theater capacity and connected depots. A border force without a safe corridor can buy local support for a monthly treasury cost when capacity allows; a more isolated force loses morale and eventually men. Connected depots spend finite stored food to support an oversized army. The state persists in saves, the Inspector checks it, and a player's own army panel explains the current condition. This is an initial Roman expedition layer; automatic long-route orders and Han-specific logistics remain to be built.
- Expert and hard AI with an active campaign objective can consider one reported closed-border theater when every legal route to a current war target is blocked. It requires a strong, well supplied army and treasury, uses the same explicit unauthorized-entry order as the player, and records a cooldown. Arrival still creates only a local border incident under the existing skirmish and diplomatic response rules; it does not silently grant transit or declare a full war. Automatic battle plans can retain command of an army, so this decision applies only to unassigned field forces.
- A player can now select a confirmed long-distance route in Map Lab and issue a persistent, leg-by-leg expedition order. Each new leg uses the existing march and attack command, pays normal transport costs, and rechecks dated control reports, passage rights, supply and physical adjacency. Changed control or insufficient transport pauses the route; a battle pauses for the player's decision, and a siege waits for resolution. The player can resume or cancel future legs, and manual field orders supersede the itinerary. Save roundtrips retain progress, while the Inspector flags malformed itineraries. Unknown control and routes needing permission cannot be ordered automatically.
- Declared civilian, commercial and diplomatic border policies now have separate access checks. Players can change their own public nonmilitary policy in Map Lab; both campaign and Map Lab allow an eligible person to travel as a civilian or a diplomatic delegation. Route previews use dated control reports, while each arrival checks actual control and current policy. Trade treaties can permit the partner's merchants without granting civilians or armies passage. Earlier saves migrate unused closed civilian/diplomatic placeholders to the previous permissive travel behavior. This is a policy and personal-travel layer; the monthly trade-income route check is described below.
- Trade treaties now dispatch one recorded goods shipment per partner per month over an actual theater-graph commercial corridor. Income reaches the recipient only after each mapped leg is traveled and its border is rechecked; a closed intermediary pauses the goods and loses them after three blocked months. Sea and difficult-terrain legs take longer, with extra winter delay. Winter sea and hill legs carry a deterministic loss risk, fixed by shipment ID and departure date so save/reload cannot reroll it. Ending the treaty cancels in-transit goods. Cargo and its route survive saves, the Inspector checks its structure, and the government panel shows only the player's own pending shipments. Trade goods are abstract regional output rather than food removed from reserves; richer commodity markets remain future work. Civilian and military passage remain separate.
- Commagene is now a locally independent microstate at the approximate Samosata site inside the broad Syrian game theater. [Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/commagene-a-portion-of-southwestern-asia-minor-modern-turkey/) documents the kingdom and Antiochus I; [Pleiades](https://pleiades.stoa.org/places/658587) supplies the site point. Its ruler has a stable local ID, while militia and resources are explicitly simulated. The Map Lab shows separate local and theater control, historical sources, delayed access/protectorate offers, siege, occupation, administration and a bounded foothold. Capturing the city does not annex the host theater or transfer legal title. Nonplayer AI can use the same contact and siege rules for autonomous cities, tribes and microstates. Older saves do not invent an unobserved Commagene history. The ruler is currently a local record rather than a full character with portrait and letters.
