## Implemented in this checkpoint

- Existing defensive coalitions can now invite a fourth or fifth country after founding. The leader sends a paid three-treasury courier only to a known court; a nonplayer court evaluates its own threat, relations and resources, and may refuse or fail to reply. A player target receives a dated mailbox letter and must explicitly accept; a second confirmation precedes membership and the five-treasury fee. The new member retains its own theaters, army, budget and government, while a membership history and contribution policy persist in saves. AI leaders can propose admissions after six months. Both campaign government and Map Lab expose player-led invitations; the Inspector warns about phantom admission records. Other organization types and the remaining charter rules remain outstanding.

- Map Lab now includes distinct Frontier and Exploration modes alongside political, organization, suzerainty, military-access, terrain and economic views. Frontier colors use only mapped land-route neighbors, the player's own theaters and dated control reports; sea routes are excluded from land-border claims. Exploration colors and its clickable list use the player's delivered local-discovery records, not undiscovered local entities. A geographical-only clue shows the theater without naming an unidentified community. Shared historical outlines get an ambiguous color when their theater statuses differ; neither mode fabricates internal borders or grants control.

- The first organization layer now builds on the existing negotiated anti-hegemon coalitions. Each founded coalition has a stable ID, editable name, founding members, charter, leader, shared threat, cohesion, contributions and departure history; existing saves gain missing charter fields. Map Lab has an organization mode where the player selects one known active coalition, sees its members over dated reported control, and can inspect membership from a theater. A player leader can rename the coalition, and a member can leave with the existing diplomatic consequences. Members retain sovereign theaters and their own armies. Unreported foreign coalitions remain hidden; reported memberships can be stale. Leadership and expulsion votes are now implemented for these defensive coalitions. Member-sponsored admissions, resource-backed assistance requests, contribution renegotiation and voluntary dissolution now operate for defensive coalitions through the council and letters. Separate peace with the common threat now records who stayed at war, lowers cohesion and relations, and exempts the peacemaker from a fresh coalition call to arms for twelve months; allies retain their own wars, armies and territory. Other kinds of leagues, richer obligations and broader organization diplomacy remain to implement.

- A deliberately fictional village in the Ravenna hinterland now demonstrates the local settlement layer without presenting invented coordinates as historical fact. Its saved record contains population, council, grain specialty, finite stored food, production, civilian need, satisfaction, security and improvement projects. Granary and irrigation construction take money, food and months; nonplayer Caesar can use the same construction rules when funds permit. Normal purchases reserve two months of civilian food; emergency requisition damages local satisfaction, security and theater loyalty. An overloaded friendly army may buy limited village support, drawing from the same finite store. A conquered village follows its host theater while retaining its prior legal title and losing any unfinished project. Map Lab exposes the village and actions; the Inspector checks its record. This is one representative village, not yet regional coverage.
- Market, well, road and wall projects now add concrete effects to that village. The market raises local revenue and permits cheaper, larger civilian-safe purchases; the well improves satisfaction and conditional population growth; the road raises local market access and the host theater's real supply capacity by one; the wall raises security and a small local militia while reducing occupation shock. Civilian food need rises with population; growth pauses without a production surplus. All projects have bounded levels, upfront resources and construction time. Existing saves containing the village gain only missing project fields, preserving population, food and ownership.
- Fields and a community shrine complete the village's initial eight-project development menu. Fields raise monthly food production; the shrine improves satisfaction at completion and during well supplied quarters. A village with at least 700 people, a granary and viable food production may qualify as a market town through a market, road, access and local satisfaction, or a fortified settlement through a wall, road and security. Market-town status adds real local revenue and one more civilian-safe food unit per purchase; a fortified settlement suffers a smaller occupation shock. These are derived local statuses that can recede; they do not create new sovereign states or change legal title.

The audit reports 24 Roman theaters, 28 routes, 15 historical polygon features, 94 Han regions and 124 Han routes. It does not equate map imagery with ownerless land.

Conquest, siege, secession and command reunification use one transfer function. Legacy saves gain default fields without changing owners or foreign reports.

- Roman courts may now propose a protectorate or client-kingdom relationship through the existing four-month agreement courier. Both are public, dated, expiring treaties: the subject keeps its theaters, armies, officials and treasury, while the suzerain owes defense. A protectorate retains independent diplomacy, full domestic autonomy and no automatic access or tribute. A client kingdom retains broad internal autonomy but has limited external war and treaty authority, pays two treasury each quarter only above a protected reserve, and grants one-way suzerain military and commercial passage. Its political loyalty and independence aspiration change with tribute performance and can lead to renunciation. A suzerain at war may request a bounded contingent from a neighboring client; 400 manpower and three food leave the subject's real reserves, travel along a mapped border with terrain delay, and reach the suzerain only on arrival. A closed or lost route blocks or loses the shipment. Multiple suzerains and circular dependencies are rejected; breach or war removes treaty rights. A proposed protector must have enough real military capacity to defend the subject, evaluated only when the recipient considers the letter. Hard and expert AI may send a client proposal to one reported nonplayer neighbor through the same courier, without immediately binding either party. The Map Lab has an own-relationship suzerainty mode based on reported theater control. Puppet regimes, Han princely institutions and wider foreign subject intelligence remain future work.
- Map Lab now has a military-access mode based on the player's reported theater controllers and its own concluded rights. It separates own control, full and limited military passage, wartime access, commercial passage without army access, closed army borders, unknown controllers and mixed historical polygons. The expandable theater list shows the date of each control report and opens the existing theater panel; the mode does not infer actual foreign control from game state. Physical routes and arrival checks remain governed by the existing movement and border rules.
- Local polities now have a separate, per-faction discovery record with unknown, geographic, contacted, identified, surveyed and documented levels. A new Roman campaign identifies its own local places, directly adjacent non-village communities and historically public Roman towns and Massilia, while a nearby village can remain only geographically known. A distant small kingdom stays present and simulated but is not named on an uninformed player's Map Lab. A successful traveler control report identifies local communities only after the return message arrives; army arrival surveys them. Discovery is monotonic and survives save upgrades, while the Inspector flags invalid records. This is the initial local-entity discovery layer; the broader world still needs more settlements, additional discovery methods and a dedicated exploration map.
- Map Lab now offers an explicit unauthorized border crossing for a known closed neighbor. On arrival, a real defending army may repel the crossing with limited losses; an undefended frontier records an unopposed crossing. Either outcome creates a persistent local incident, harms relations and credibility, and does not automatically declare war or transfer the theater. The player log reveals own losses without giving exact foreign casualties.
- A player involved in an incident can resolve it from a Map Lab panel. The crossing side can withdraw, pay compensation and withdraw, or seek one-way local passage. The defending side can protest, tolerate, or explicitly escalate through the normal war declaration system. The Inspector checks incident identities, geography and losses; the panel does not reveal exact foreign casualties.
- AI defenders protest an actual crossing after observation, while AI attackers respond to a protest with compensation or withdrawal according to resources and relations. The player is not auto-committed to a response. Both sides use the same incident-resolution functions.
