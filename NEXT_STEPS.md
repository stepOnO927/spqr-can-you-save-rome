# SPQR — remaining work

This tracker records the state of the **independent development branch**. A working mechanic is not a claim that every phase directive has been fulfilled. The 49 BCE Roman campaign remains the main playable scenario.

## Implemented and checked

- **Phase XI reconnaissance:** scouts, probes, waiting, dated and uncertain reports, prebattle estimates, battle reviews, a War Council, neutral military density markers, and later campaign AI scouting. The Senate Standard 5,200-man opening is covered in English and Chinese browser smoke tests.
- **Phase XIV development:** two research branches with 34 material innovations and 25 national ideas, 59 distinct interpretive vector illustrations, five idea categories, construction, institutional slot expansion, knowledge purchase/sale/exchange, visiting scholars, and local religion policy. Roads, bridges, harbor storage, and coastal forts have simulation effects. Senate and Egypt have automated 36-month grand-campaign checks using opposed five-idea strategies. AI states can sell knowledge to each other and send four-month offers to the player; accepting, declining and expiry are save-compatible and never charge the player without consent. Guarded military methods require stronger relations in purchases, sales, and exchanges. All currently modeled idea and building effect keys have English and Chinese labels, including naval effects.
- **Phase XVIII naval core:** fleets, shipbuilding, officer assignments, sea combat, piracy, transport, maritime trade, and AI fleet orders. Patrols can fight pirate vessels; amphibious attacks use a disclosed battle penalty. AI can embark troops with actual transport ships. Trade payments now depend on cargo quantity, route length and scarcity, while raids move captured goods into the raider's inventory. A hostile blockade reduces an army's supply in the blockaded port. Docked fleets can be put into reserve for lower upkeep, but cannot carry armies or cargo, take orders, or fight at full readiness. Recommissioning costs 6 treasury and 2 food and takes a full month; save reloads and port capture preserve the resulting state. Fleets can choose formation, ramming, boarding or missile tactics, which change battle power and losses. Boarding can capture ships and named commanders; captives can be ransomed and return after travel time. AI now estimates its own naval cash pressure, lays up unaffordable docked fleets, recalls costly patrols before doing so, and recommissions in response to dated hostile sightings using the same resource and time costs. The actions, battle reports, and old-save defaults are covered by tests.
- **Legacy achievements:** silent in active campaigns, revealed on formal conclusion or terminal defeat, with one-time reward keys. New campaigns have unique IDs; older imported saves receive a stable ID based on the imported file.
- **Regression:** `npm test` and browser smoke scripts for the atlas, Senate reconnaissance, development, and grand campaign.
- **Offline atlas:** MapLibre GL JS, its license, physical land and sea data, and 445 terrain tiles are bundled. A disconnected browser smoke test loads 120 settlement markers with a real map engine. The whole playable Mediterranean has terrain detail through zoom 6, with Italy and the Adriatic through zoom 8; zoom 9 and higher remain network-dependent. The overview campaign map uses a clearer sea and land wash, stronger faction tints, a lighter coastline edge, and subdued terrain marks in political mode.
- **Grand-campaign start fix:** Starting the tutorial or free play inside the atlas now creates the 120-region campaign, matching the main entry flow. Base-map city labels are suppressed in grand mode; the tutorial's highlighted city remains available. Major settlement labels have a darker, more legible cartographic style.
- **Pirate cargo:** A successful pirate interception now removes actual goods from the source, stores them in the pirate band's save-compatible cargo inventory, and lets a successful patrol recover one unit where storage allows. Reports show seized and recovered goods in both languages. Reappointing an already assigned fleet commander no longer charges a second fee.
- **Phase XII campaign resolution:** Strategic objectives remain reversible and do not trigger victory. Both Roman commands can win through decisive military collapse or a favorable civil settlement that ends the war, changes control, updates government recognition and improves political standing. Victory can lead to continued rule without exposing achievements. Formal endings save a bilingual campaign summary, a reviewable chronicle and one-time Legacy rewards. The battle review now names initial friendly strength, intelligence age, relevant terrain, supply and command lessons, and the actual outcome. Both views show a budget derived from economic formulas; army training charges 8 treasury and 2 food and occupies one month. Six Caesar/Senate strategy simulations of up to 36 months and browser checks cover the new flow.
- **Opposing marches:** Hostile armies that cross the same land border in opposite directions now fight a meeting engagement instead of exchanging territories without combat. Neither side receives fortification cover or free occupation; each receives a dated, approximate battle observation for the territory it attempted to enter. The bilingual battle report names the encounter, and player casualty logs use the player's actual side. Opening-year AI now considers fresh battle intelligence when deciding whether to repeat an attack. Save reload and this behavior have regression coverage.

## Still incomplete

1. **Phase XIV source and art review.** Every technology card has a different original schematic drawing, not an excavated artifact. Sixteen material cards and three idea cards now link directly to checked sources; the review corrected the misleading fortification and library labels, added period-specific evidence for drainage, aqueducts, baths, grain storage, supply depots, milling, tax farming, estate management, and rhetorical practice, and kept save IDs. See `TECH_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`. The other 18 material cards and 22 ideas still need a direct source review. The 34/25 catalog is a playable slice; deeper policy, religious, cultural, and civilization-specific content remains unfinished.
2. **Phase XIV balance and long campaigns.** A no-order Caesar run on the 120-region map reached terminal defeat after 22 months in both desktop and mobile browser audits; this is an observed outcome, not a balance target. Senate and Egypt complete automated 36-month grand-campaign development paths. More manual play across factions and difficulties is needed without scripting a guaranteed result. Research, economy, faith, migration, and diplomacy need longer campaign review.
3. **Phase XVIII naval depth.** Piracy and sea battles still use aggregate fleet calculations. Player tactical choices and commander capture now work, but a broader port-to-front maritime supply network and more nuanced detection remain to be built and balanced. Trade prices, tactical choices and AI troop transports also need long campaign balance testing.
4. **Phase XV map geography and visual review.** The 120 territories are gameplay cells based on imperfect historical sources. Duplicate base-city labels are hidden in grand campaign mode. The grand atlas now draws broad faction areas rather than the synthetic city-cell mesh, while city labels carry live faction accents and cell hover still supports selection; Italy terrain is bundled through zoom 8. An offline 200 m Natural Earth depth band now distinguishes the continental shelf from deeper water; 1,000–3,000 m bands remain visible. Overview and zoomed 1440-pixel screenshots were inspected in a real browser. Coastlines, borders, names, terrain, contrast, zoom behavior, small screens, and accessibility still need a deeper cartographic review and user acceptance. This remains the user's principal visual complaint.
5. **Phase XVI Legacy hub depth.** The city, shop, museum, archive, race, and campaign rewards are playable, but building-specific activities and museum curation are narrower than the full directive.
6. **Phase XI continued playtesting.** One Senate Standard path and both languages are covered by browser smoke, plus targeted simulation tests. The atlas tutorial and free start now retain the grand campaign. More independent human campaigns, alternate decisions, long intelligence chains, AI mistakes, and save/import flows need playtesting. Local saves contain actual simulation state, so fog of war is a gameplay interface boundary, not file encryption.
7. **Historical audit.** Figures, politics, religion, regional boundaries, and economic values are approximations. Check source and license records before presenting any additional historical statement as fact.
8. **Phase XII balance depth.** With opposing marches resolved as battles, the current six-strategy audit shows Caesarian aggressive play ending in defeat after 24 months at 9 territories and -100 treasury; defensive and low-recruitment play end in defeat after 17 months. A 36-month Egypt-led run also saw AI Caesar lose its field armies and reach zero territory around month 22; its later -37 treasury was a symptom alongside military collapse, not the sole cause. Senate aggressive play ended at 80 territories and 541 treasury; Senate no-order play ended at 67 territories and 677 treasury. These are observed outcomes, not balance targets. The formerly reported aggressive Caesarian survival to month 36 is no longer current. An earlier paired simulation showed that reserving the opening fleet can improve Caesarian treasury, but both paths still lost their armies. Army, tax, supply and regional opening values need a deliberate balance pass; a trial of earlier AI scouting made the Caesarian audit worse and was reverted. The budget projects regular costs; one-time orders, trade, repairs, battles and events can change the actual month. More manual long-run tests and defeat-screen review are needed.
