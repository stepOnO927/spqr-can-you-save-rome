# SPQR — remaining work

This tracker records the state of the **independent development branch**. A working mechanic is not a claim that every phase directive has been fulfilled. The 49 BCE Roman campaign remains the main playable scenario.

## Implemented and checked

- **Provincial rites now affect neighboring alliances:** An active local-rites policy gives +5 alliance credit, while civic patronage gives −5, only when a directly adjoining province has an influential priesthood and dominant tradition shared with the other court but not the actor. An unfinished policy transition gives no credit. The credit is recomputed from live land control, beliefs and priesthood influence; it does not silently alter relation scores or bypass war and incompatible alliances. The Senate–Numidia case moves from an ineligible 25 to an eligible 30 after the paid three-month policy change, while distant or same-tradition courts receive none. The policy and calculation survive save reload. English/Chinese map diplomacy and religion panels, actual alliance execution, all 460 tests, the full Phase XIV browser smoke and twelve 72-month observer campaigns passed; those campaigns remain seven Senate victories and five unresolved wars. [Cicero, *In Verrem* 2.4.113–115](https://www.attalus.org/cicero/verres24_3.html) supports political concern over local sacred practice, but no specific 49 BCE court response or numerical bonus; see `TECH_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`.
- **Supply collapse is final and recorded:** A locally unsupplied one-person unit now loses its last soldier instead of surviving forever through rounding. The engine removes armies after both national food and local supply attrition, records player losses with their cause in the campaign chronicle, and sends an attached living commander through the existing three-month missing-person recovery flow. English and Chinese loss messages, save reload and terminal defeat are covered by targeted tests. All 459 tests, Phase XII browser smoke, Senate Standard bilingual reconnaissance browser smoke, seven 36-month campaign routes and the 48-month player-like Caesar route pass. The 72-month twelve-seed result remains seven Senate victories and five ongoing wars.
- **Frontier coverage counts available defenders:** A force besieging enemy-held land or already moving does not count as a friendly frontier garrison when an outnumbered AI chooses where to pay for its next levy. In a controlled lost-Narbo case, the AI raises the levy on its exposed Narbo border instead of reinforcing an unrelated core army; the order and cost survive save reload. The long-run outcome distribution is unchanged from the previous frontier-reinforcement audit.
- **AI frontier reinforcement:** A territorially outnumbered AI now spends its ordinary levy or reinforcement resources at the least-covered hostile land frontier, instead of choosing the first border cell in the map data. This lowers the number of live DOM markers near a city, but the 1920 × 1080 software-renderer benchmark still reads about 3.3 pan and 3.7 zoom animation callbacks per second with relief enabled. It does not establish a performance improvement. The broader draw cost and visual treatment of the flat Egyptian coast remain unfinished.

The atlas tutorial and free start now retain the grand campaign. A no-order main-view campaign reached a terminal result after 19 months on both 1440-pixel desktop and 390-pixel mobile browser sizes with no script errors or horizontal overflow. A separate 48-month Senate Standard simulation made cautious decisions from visible intelligence, fought six battles and reloaded three times. Another 48-month Caesar route used dated scouting, reinforcement and three reloads, fought six battles, captured Rome, but still ended at -100 treasury and -95 food with no civil-war conclusion. These divergent, unresolved outcomes expose the need for more interactive human campaigns, alternate decisions, AI mistakes, economic balance and save/import flows. Local saves contain actual simulation state, so fog of war is a gameplay interface boundary, not file encryption.
7. **Historical audit.** Figures, politics, religion, regional boundaries, and economic values are approximations. Check source and license records before presenting any additional historical statement as fact.
8. **Phase XII balance depth.** AI armies consolidate after siege captures even when the player is a third party. A territorially outnumbered AI can pay the usual 24 treasury, 6 food and 3,000 manpower for a city garrison after a capture; the new levy cannot march in its recruitment turn and will hold the city while it remains owned, at war and adequately supplied. Low-cash AI fleets stay in reserve after a hostile sighting unless the treasury covers three projected deficit months and an operating reserve. We now also test replacement under general wartime pressure and use the common retreat rule for low-supply AI. The budget now exposes actual one-time construction and research payments, but this does not settle accumulation or expenditure balance. The opening Caesarian reserve and wartime spending priority remain, but their earlier five-seed comparison was invalidated by silent ordinary peace in four seeds. With the later research-choice changes, Caesar holds 20, 11, 13, 22 and 15 territories after 36 months in the same five-seed spectator audit; four reach formal Senate settlements and one remains at war. Caesar aggressive play still ends in defeat; the player reinforced Italy route remains ongoing at month 36. Caesar starts with 28 territories, 180 treasury and a projected -13 monthly change with its fleet active; Senate starts with 72, 135 treasury and +18. A trial that delayed optional AI research while an outnumbered command had a projected deficit still ended in twelve Senate victories across twelve seeds and was reverted. Further resource, AI strategy, human play and defeat-screen review are required.

The latest Phase XII balance pass traced one repeatable failure: Caesar could take Rome with roughly 6,000 field troops but had too little treasury to recruit a garrison, while an earlier paid-garrison experiment starved the army. A shared wartime municipal-contribution action now raises a one-time sum of at most 40 treasury from a bounded set of loyal, reasonably satisfied owned settlements, costs local loyalty, satisfaction and national stability, and has a 12-month cooldown retained in saves. The AI uses the same action when its wartime cash flow is negative and its treasury is nearly empty. Before paying for a city garrison, it now checks whether six months of projected food can support the additional 3,000 troops. This is a game decision inspired by [Caesar's partisan account of municipal contributions in *Civil War* 1.6.8](https://classics.andrewgadsden.com/library/caesar/de-bello-civili/1?line=76), not evidence for a particular revenue amount or public reaction. Browser tests cover both campaign views, English, Simplified Chinese and saved cooldowns; the chronicle describes the action in both languages. The capped twelve-seed, 72-month observer audit ends in two Senate victories and ten ongoing wars, with no Caesar victory. This reduces the earlier one-sided result but increases long stalemates; Caesar's 48-month player route still reaches -100 treasury and -95 food. Strategic AI, supply and settlement balance remain unfinished.
