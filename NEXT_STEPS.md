# SPQR — remaining work

This tracker records the state of the **independent development branch**. A working mechanic is not a claim that every phase directive has been fulfilled. The 49 BCE Roman campaign remains the main playable scenario.

## Implemented and checked

- **Zoomed Italy locator:** At zoom 7 and above, an inset derived from the bundled Natural Earth coastline shows the map center against the Italian peninsula and the Tyrrhenian and Adriatic seas. It hides outside Italy and on narrow screens. A real offline MapLibre browser check covers zoom 4–9 terrain, the inset position, both languages, and the 1440-pixel header fit; 390-pixel mobile still passes. The modern coastline is labeled as orientation, not a reconstructed ancient border.
- **Phase XI reconnaissance:** scouts, probes, waiting, dated and uncertain reports, prebattle estimates, battle reviews, a War Council, neutral military density markers, and later campaign AI scouting. The Senate Standard 5,200-man opening is covered in English and Chinese browser smoke tests.
- **Military map legend cleanup:** An obsolete fallback still described red and amber strength tiers even though the live atlas rendered neutral ivory counters. It has been removed, and the bilingual Senate browser test checks that the military legend uses no faction-color strength swatches.
- **Phase XIV development:** two research branches with 34 material innovations and 28 national ideas, 62 distinct interpretive vector illustrations, five idea categories, construction, institutional slot expansion, knowledge purchase/sale/exchange, visiting scholars, and local religion policy. Roads, bridges, harbor storage, and coastal forts have simulation effects. Senate and Egypt have automated 36-month grand-campaign checks using opposed five-idea strategies. AI states can sell knowledge to each other and send four-month offers to the player; accepting, declining and expiry are save-compatible and never charge the player without consent. Guarded military methods require stronger relations in purchases, sales, and exchanges. All currently modeled idea and building effect keys have English and Chinese labels, including naval effects.
- **Phase XVIII naval core:** fleets, shipbuilding, officer assignments, sea combat, piracy, transport, maritime trade, and AI fleet orders. Patrols can fight pirate vessels; amphibious attacks use a disclosed battle penalty. AI can embark troops with actual transport ships. Trade payments now depend on cargo quantity, route length and scarcity, while raids move captured goods into the raider's inventory. A hostile blockade reduces an army's supply in the blockaded port. Docked fleets can be put into reserve for lower upkeep, but cannot carry armies or cargo, take orders, or fight at full readiness. Recommissioning costs 6 treasury and 2 food and takes a full month; save reloads and port capture preserve the resulting state. Fleets can choose formation, ramming, boarding or missile tactics, which change battle power and losses. Boarding can capture ships and named commanders; captives can be ransomed and return after travel time. AI now estimates its own naval cash pressure, lays up unaffordable docked fleets, recalls costly patrols before doing so, and recommissions in response to dated hostile sightings using the same resource and time costs. The actions, battle reports, and old-save defaults are covered by tests.
- **Legacy achievements:** silent in active campaigns, revealed on formal conclusion or terminal defeat, with one-time reward keys. New campaigns have unique IDs; older imported saves receive a stable ID based on the imported file.
- **Regression:** `npm test` and browser smoke scripts for the atlas, Senate reconnaissance, development, and grand campaign.
- **Offline atlas:** MapLibre GL JS, its license, physical land and sea data, and 1,173 terrain tiles are bundled. A disconnected browser smoke test loads 120 settlement markers with a real map engine. The whole playable Mediterranean has terrain detail through zoom 6, with Italy and the Adriatic through zoom 9, the atlas's maximum useful zoom. The overview campaign map uses a clearer sea and land wash, stronger faction tints, a lighter coastline edge, and subdued terrain marks in political mode.
- **Atlas relief and mobile layout:** A clearer elevation palette now separates lowlands, hills and mountains, and zoom-dependent crossfades retire coarse terrain tiles as finer bundled tiles take over. The atlas now draws rank-styled Natural Earth 1:10m rivers and natural lakes instead of only 1:110m major-river lines; a browser screenshot shows the Tevere between Roma and Ostia. Modern canals and reservoirs are excluded from this ancient-campaign visual reference, and both language legends explain the new water color. At 390 and 320 pixels wide, the overview starts folded, the command panel spans the usable width, and the save transfer controls sit outside the scrolling top menu. The tutorial moves its instruction card away from the current target so the army and month controls remain clickable. Grand-campaign settlement labels now retain MapLibre's absolute positioning; Ariminum's marker sits at its map coordinate instead of hundreds of screen pixels into the Adriatic. Real offline browser renders and the Senate Standard reconnaissance path passed on desktop and both small widths in English and Chinese. More art and geography review is still needed.
- **Offline map export:** The screenshot button now loads its licensed image compositor from local game files. With all external browser requests blocked, the button generated a 1920 × 1080 PNG with detailed map pixels and triggered the named download. The managed test browser cancels even trivial file downloads, so saving the file to disk still needs an ordinary browser check.
- **Grand-campaign start fix:** Starting the tutorial or free play inside the atlas now creates the 120-region campaign, matching the main entry flow. Base-map city labels are suppressed in grand mode; the tutorial's highlighted city remains available. Major settlement labels have a darker, more legible cartographic style.
- **Pirate cargo:** A successful pirate interception now removes actual goods from the source, stores them in the pirate band's save-compatible cargo inventory, and lets a successful patrol recover one unit where storage allows. Reports show seized and recovered goods in both languages. Reappointing an already assigned fleet commander no longer charges a second fee.
- **Phase XII campaign resolution:** Strategic objectives remain reversible and do not trigger victory. Both Roman commands can win through decisive military collapse or a favorable civil settlement that ends the war, changes control, updates government recognition and improves political standing. Victory can lead to continued rule without exposing achievements. Formal endings save a bilingual campaign summary, a reviewable chronicle and one-time Legacy rewards. The battle review now names initial friendly strength, intelligence age, relevant terrain, supply and command lessons, and the actual outcome. Both views show a budget derived from economic formulas. Its bilingual action-cost note now distinguishes one-time construction and research payments from monthly upkeep and states the actual levy, reinforcement and training costs instead of displaying fictitious zero monthly charges. Seven Caesar/Senate strategy simulations of up to 36 months and browser checks cover the new flow.
- **Opposing marches:** Hostile armies that cross the same land border in opposite directions now fight a meeting engagement instead of exchanging territories without combat. Neither side receives fortification cover or free occupation; each receives a dated, approximate battle observation for the territory it attempted to enter. The bilingual battle report names the encounter, and player casualty logs use the player's actual side. Opening-year AI now considers fresh battle intelligence when deciding whether to repeat an attack. Save reload and this behavior have regression coverage.
- **AI emergency levy:** A command that has fewer than 1,500 field troops while at war can raise a 3,000-man force outside its normal four-month recruitment cycle if it still owns territory and has the same treasury, food, and manpower required of the player. This prevents an otherwise solvent AI state from waiting passively after its last army is destroyed; the 36-month audit shows it does not by itself solve the Caesarian balance problem.
- **AI battlefield caution:** AI armies now pause to consolidate a theater they just captured, and depleted forces under 1,200 men seek a safer adjacent friendly theater rather than remaining exposed or launching an offensive. Armies waiting for reconnaissance are not simultaneously given a march order. These decisions use current territory, supply and the AI's own battle state; they do not read concealed enemy force counts. Targeted tests and the seven-strategy audit cover the behavior.
- **AI border reinforcement:** When an existing grand-campaign army is eligible at the rally point, the AI now orders the same paid, one-month 3,000-man reinforcement available to the player before issuing march orders. Alert and scheduled recruitment cannot both raise units at a theater already awaiting reinforcement. An already assembled force of at least 6,000 men does not automatically attract a further levy at that theater. Targeted tests verify the resource cost, delay, added men and absence of a duplicate levy. Wider opening balance still needs review.
- **Finite maritime army supply:** A docked fleet with transports or merchants can load food from its faction stock, sail through actual sea zones, and unload at a friendly port unless a hostile blockade prevents it. Land supply travels through connected land territories; thin local port forage is capped, while delivered food creates a finite stock that is consumed each month. Cancellation, ship losses, return trips, port capture and old saves have defined outcomes. The fleet panel shows cargo in transit and stocked ports in English and Chinese. AI fleets use their own army supply reading to send paid convoys to thinly supplied fronts when cash and food permit. A 36-month audit confirmed that the port-food threshold and this limited AI behavior preserve the previous fixed-seed Caesar and Senate outcomes; this is a regression check, not a balance solution.

## Still incomplete

1. **Phase XIV source and art review.** Every technology card has a different original schematic drawing, not an excavated artifact. All 34 material cards and 28 idea cards now have a direct source review in `TECH_SOURCE_AUDIT.md`. The latest pass added public legal reference and allied citizen registration with distinct art, actual upkeep and loyalty or manpower effects, and explicit limits on what Diodorus and Cicero prove. Earlier source corrections retain save IDs. Provincial sanctuary protection now raises quarterly satisfaction where an influential local priesthood represents a different dominant tradition, with monthly upkeep. Mutually exclusive ideas can now replace one another in their own slot without an impossible intermediate removal, while other active slots still enforce conflicts and prevent duplicate effects; English and Chinese browser paths cover the replacement. Some cards remain explicit policy abstractions rather than documented 49 BCE institutions. Negative idea effects now display a minus sign in both languages. The reviewed 34/28 catalog still does not exhaust the requested policy, religious, cultural, or civilization-specific content; that content remains unfinished.
2. **Phase XIV balance and long campaigns.** A no-order Caesar run on the 120-region map reached terminal defeat after 22 months in both desktop and mobile browser audits; this is an observed outcome, not a balance target. Senate and Egypt complete automated 36-month grand-campaign development paths. More manual play across factions and difficulties is needed without scripting a guaranteed result. Research, economy, faith, migration, and diplomacy need longer campaign review.
3. **Phase XVIII naval depth.** Piracy and sea battles still use aggregate fleet calculations. Player tactical choices, commander capture and finite maritime supply convoys work; AI has a limited supply need trigger. Richer port-to-front logistics, convoy prioritization under competing naval threats and more nuanced detection remain to be built and balanced. Trade prices, tactical choices and AI troop transports also need long campaign balance testing.
4. **Phase XV map geography and visual review.** The 120 territories are gameplay cells based on imperfect historical sources. Duplicate base-city labels are hidden in grand campaign mode. The grand atlas draws broad faction areas rather than the synthetic city-cell mesh, while city labels carry live faction accents and cell hover still supports selection; Italy terrain is now bundled through zoom 9. An offline 200 m Natural Earth depth band distinguishes the continental shelf from deeper water; 1,000–3,000 m bands remain visible. Rank-styled 1:10m river lines and natural lake polygons now add detail across the Mediterranean. Updated overview, zoomed and mobile screenshots have been inspected in a real browser. The CSS marker-position defect is fixed and a browser assertion checks Ariminum stays at the focused coordinate. A modern-coastline audit checks all 120 site anchors and finds only Iol and Iader just offshore, by 0.6 km and 0.3 km; it fails if a future anchor lies more than 2 km offshore, while leaving ancient coastline uncertainty explicit. The higher-resolution elevation and water data improve geographic fidelity, but the map can still look soft and sparse at very close zoom; coastlines, borders, names, terrain, contrast, zoom behavior, accessibility and user acceptance still need deeper cartographic review. This remains the user's principal visual complaint.
5. **Phase XVI Legacy hub depth.** The city, shop, museum, archive, race, and campaign rewards are playable, but building-specific activities and museum curation are narrower than the full directive.
6. **Phase XI continued playtesting.** One Senate Standard path and both languages are covered by browser smoke, plus targeted simulation tests. The atlas tutorial and free start now retain the grand campaign. A no-order main-view campaign reached a terminal result after 19 months on both 1440-pixel desktop and 390-pixel mobile browser sizes with no script errors or horizontal overflow. More independent human campaigns, alternate decisions, long intelligence chains, AI mistakes, and save/import flows need playtesting. Local saves contain actual simulation state, so fog of war is a gameplay interface boundary, not file encryption.
7. **Historical audit.** Figures, politics, religion, regional boundaries, and economic values are approximations. Check source and license records before presenting any additional historical statement as fact.
8. **Phase XII balance depth.** With opposing marches, AI consolidation, withdrawal and one-month border reinforcement, the current fixed-seed seven-strategy plus Egypt spectator audit shows Caesarian aggressive play ending in defeat at month 24 with 9 territories and -100 treasury; defensive and low-recruitment play both end around August 48 BCE. The AI now pays for reinforcements after an exposed victory when territorially outnumbered, scouts nearby hostile land, uses a food-cost probe when short of cash, and may accept a risky attack after a fresh inconclusive report. These decisions use its own army, treasury, public territory control and received reports; they do not inspect concealed enemy troops. The Egypt spectator improved from zero to six Caesar territories at month 36, while the player reinforced route declined from 31 to 26, so the balance issue is not resolved. A Caesar path that reserves its costly fleet, takes northern Italy, requests two real one-month reinforcements for Legio XIII, then marches toward Rome and southern Italy remains ongoing at month 36 with Rome, southern Italy, 26 territories, 65 treasury and 13 food. This is one playable route under one fixed audit seed, not proof of generally fair balance. An Egypt-led 36-month run leaves AI Caesar at 6 territories and Senate at 94; the Roman AI needs a better strategic counter to the Senate's resource advantage. At the opening, the budget projects Caesar at -13 treasury per month with an active fleet and Senate at +18; reserving the fleet reduces Caesar's recurring naval bill, but that choice alone does not save a no-order campaign. Senate aggressive play ended at 83 territories and 599 treasury; Senate no-order play ended at 66 territories and 666 treasury. These are observed outcomes, not balance targets. Army, tax, supply and regional opening values need a deliberate balance pass. The budget projects regular costs; one-time orders, trade, repairs, battles and events can change the actual month. More manual long-run tests and defeat-screen review are needed.
