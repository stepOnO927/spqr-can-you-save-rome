# Multipolar power struggle: current implementation

## Operational intelligence and its limits

Allied offensive objectives are no longer read from foreign live battle plans. The planner can prefer a common objective only after a dated operational notice reaches its own court, while its own control reports still identify an enemy there. The notice preserves the sender's objectives at dispatch; it never reveals later changes or cancellation. It expires after six months or loses planning eligibility when the relationship or the recipient's own war changes. A delivered intention does not prove that troops have departed, that an operation remains active, or that joint action is agreed.

The existing mail courier carries notices, including route-based travel and possible loss. A notice costs one treasury. Courts can send at most one per month and cannot notify the same partner again for six months. AI courts can send their own notices to full, offensive or defensive treaty partners without human intervention. They neither issue orders to another faction nor automatically declare war. The player has a confirmed, paid action under Operations for approved offensive and siege plans, and reads incoming notices in the ordinary mailbox. The sent list never exposes the private delivery result.

`test/operation-intelligence.test.js` checks hidden-plan invariance, courier delay and loss, snapshot persistence, expiry, eligibility and autonomous dispatch. `tools/operation-notice-smoke.mjs` exercises the player confirmation, cost, saved pending notice, English display and delivered notice in the atlas mailbox. This notice remains a communication channel for independent planning. Consented departure coordination uses the separate three-letter process below; conditional frontier peace concessions are described below.

The strategic review uses each actor's own resources, frontier control reports, dated sightings, known public wars, stability reports, treaty commitments, leader disposition and political memory. It does not read foreign army strengths, finances or secret plans. A missing sighting is not treated as proof of an undefended frontier. Reviews are bounded to eight candidates and at most one new aggressive decision per twelve months; a newly observed war can trigger an earlier review.

AI can react to a reported third-party war, sometimes pursue an opportunistic war or breach a non-aggression treaty, negotiate aid via existing agreements, support reported claimants and send captive-release letters to other AI courts. AI courts may spend 5 treasury to ask a known partner to join a war. The outbound and return journeys each take two months and may be lost; the recipient independently evaluates its own information and may refuse. Human players are not automatically committed. The recipient's existing diplomacy rules and resources matter; the supported faction remains autonomous. AI-only tests use the explicit `tick(..., {autoplayPlayer: true})` option; normal gameplay still requires the human player's decision on incoming offers.

The Chessboard's Multipolar Power Network projects reported conflicts, public agreements, dated troop sightings and possible shared threats inferred from reported wars; a shared threat is not proof of a coalition. It does not render foreign strategic plans or unreported secret treaties. Frontier war reports and later reports that a known war ended enter the Chronicle when the observer has contact; old reports may remain stale.

A spy can investigate secret agreements, producing a dated, unverified lead identifying one reported partner or no reliable information. A partner can forward its own existing lead through the ordinary delayed exchange. The power network displays delivered leads without disclosing treaty terms or asserting that they remain active; no finding is not evidence of absence. AI courts may make some defensive proposals secret, and these AI-to-AI agreement proposals now use the same delayed, fallible courier process as other agreement letters. This does not provide full clandestine negotiation or prove all desired strategic chains.

National-focus treaty effects also send proposals through the courier. When an AI writes to the human player, the letter requires an explicit decision; acceptance sends a reply, and the treaty forms only if a later confirmation arrives. Either journey may fail and refusal or silence creates no treaty. AI-only mode continues to make its own decisions. A reverse focus cannot silently send a letter or spend funds on behalf of a human player.

## Validation and limits

Run `node scripts/multipolar-sim.mjs` for six seeds across the ordinary all-AI opening, a passive-Rome game and a fictional regional-tension opening. No additional war or alliance is forced. In the present 24-month sample the ordinary openings did not produce an opportunistic declaration; the pressure opening produced both declarations and non-declarations. This does not establish that all requested chain reactions or diplomacy actions are implemented.

The current implementation can coordinate a future month for releasing each court's own approved orders. It does not combine allied armies in one battle or guarantee simultaneous arrivals, and does not provide general territorial demands or attack-linked concessions. Named-war neutrality supplies the fixed commitment described below. It does not infer an empty garrison or full supply-line status from missing observations. Wider AI-only diplomacy, event-driven reevaluation of every kind of intelligence, long-run balance, betrayal and mediation chains need further work and validation. Existing save data is retained; plans initialize lazily.

## Conditional territorial peace offers

New ordinary, term-free peace offers also record their current war generation. A delivered acceptance for an ended conflict cannot end a later war, and a known obsolete proposal no longer prevents a fresh inquiry. The mailbox marks stale received offers and removes reply controls; the government panel offers only current-war decisions. A reply is checked against its exact received letter before dispatching the diplomatic answer, so an old letter cannot answer a newer request from the same court. Older term-free records without a war identity retain legacy compatibility; the game does not invent missing historical war numbers. `test/peace-war-identity.test.js` and `tools/peace-war-identity-smoke.mjs` cover the new binding and old-record compatibility.

The existing peace inquiry can include a proposal to transfer one owned frontier theater, identified using the sender's own control reports. The modeled command core, Rome and the last owned theater cannot be offered. This transfers simulated military control, not a claim that the Roman civil-war commands are separate sovereign empires. It is an offer in return for peace, not a demand, an attack subsidy or a general territorial negotiation system.

Both map views provide a confirmed proposal with the ordinary inquiry fee and round-trip estimate. Incoming offers and replies show the terms. A receiver decides independently from its own condition, estimates and the static value of the offered theater; it does not inspect the sender's hidden ownership or forces. A threatened AI may include a low-value frontier concession in its ordinary annual peace approach, with or without an automated Roman player.

No territory changes at dispatch or acceptance. A delivered acceptance ends the war and transfers control together only if the sender still owns the theater, keeps another theater, and the recipient remains adjacent. A different war generation or replacement court invalidates the old terms. Lost or refused replies transfer nothing. Old term-free peace letters retain their existing behavior. The transfer removes invalid local appointments and neutral sieges, changes future economy through ordinary ownership, and does not change army allegiance or teleport troops. A dated transfer record stays on the original inquiry.

`test/territorial-peace.test.js` covers restrictions, fee, save/load, acceptance/refusal/loss, private-state isolation, changed circumstances, military withdrawal and AI offers. `tools/territorial-peace-smoke.mjs` exercises proposals in both views and the player's acceptance of an incoming concession. Cloud gates must pass before merging this feature; these fixtures do not establish long-run balance or complete every form of diplomatic bargaining.

## Evidence-triggered reviews

Between quarterly reviews, a court also reevaluates when its own reported stability, strength estimates or troop sightings change, when it learns of a war, when one of its own agreements changes, or when its own army positions or broad resource bands change. The signature is built from the court's own information and bounded recent records; hidden foreign changes alone cannot trigger a review. This does not yet cover every kind of intelligence or deliver the full multipolar acceptance scenario.

Expiration of a recent sighting or stability report also triggers review when it crosses the relevant age threshold. Changes in the court's own leadership, strategic personality or current leaders' political memories trigger a review as well; another court's private memories do not. Age-related gradual decay remains covered by the ordinary quarterly review.

A change in the reported controller of a neighboring theater also triggers a review. The signature uses the observer's control reports, so an unobserved change in a foreign theater does not wake the planner or disclose its controller. Changes in the court's own relations or alliances and in its delivered diplomatic reports also trigger review; private agreements between other courts do not.

## Coalition invitations

Courts select prospective partners from their own known contacts rather than reading the candidates' private threat assessments. An invitation costs the sender 3 treasury, travels for two months and can be lost or declined. AI recipients decide from their own resources, relations and perceived danger. The player can send invitations from the government panel, or receive and answer AI invitations in the mailbox. A human answer travels back for two months; if the player accepted, a separate confirmation then takes another two months and may be lost. Only a delivered, successful confirmation forms the coalition and charges each member 5 treasury; acceptance itself does not declare war. The existing coalition system handles membership and defense obligations, and invitation history is saved with a twelve-month repeat limit.

## Wartime aid between AI courts

An AI government at war can pay five treasury to ask a known, non-hostile court for aid, including a court it has heard is already fighting the same enemy. The recipient independently weighs its own treasury, food, relations and wars; it may refuse. An accepted shipment deducts 12 treasury and 8 food from the donor, travels with the return courier, and can be lost; only a delivered shipment reaches the requester. The request and sealed response do not expose the donor's reserves to other countries. Incoming requests reach the human player’s mailbox after travel. The player can confirm the stated 12 treasury and 8 food cost, decline, or leave the request unanswered; no aid is deducted automatically. A player at war can also send an aid request from either map view, subject to the same contact and courier rules; lost outbound requests remain pending until an expected reply fails to arrive. The seeded simulation includes a Roman-Egyptian war both with autonomous Rome and with a passive human Rome; only the initial war is set, not the later diplomatic outcomes.

## Observed frontier pressure

Non-Roman AI courts at peace may place an idle army already at home on a temporary defensive stance when their own recent sighting reports a significant hostile force on or next to that frontier. They do not inspect unseen forces, override existing orders or operational plans, move forces, declare war, or issue orders to a human-controlled army. The stance ends if war begins, when the report is stale or corrected, or after two months; a cooldown gives the army time to pursue other goals. Wartime and Roman commanders retain their existing planning. This adds a bounded reaction, not full tactical coordination or proof that every desired multi-state chain occurs.

## Scout-based allied warnings

`strategic-warnings.js` lets a court forward a fresh (at most one month old) scout sighting of a hostile force near a friendly court's territory. A friendly court is one with an alliance, a defensive, guarantee, intelligence, military-support or offensive agreement, or a relation of at least 35 with the sender. The threat must be at war with the sender, or the sender must hold a dated report of a war between the threat and the recipient. The sender reads only its own sightings, control reports, diplomatic reports, treaties and relations.

A warning costs the sender 1 treasury, is limited to one letter per court per month and is not repeated for the same army within six months. It travels for half of the existing round-trip courier time (at least one month), may be delayed once or lost, and expires if the parties go to war, the recipient loses its territory or the sighting is more than six months old on arrival. On delivery it is stored as a dated second-hand sighting (`source: 'ally_warning'`) that never replaces a newer first-hand report.

An AI recipient judges the letter from its own nearby forces, its relation with the sender, the sender's credibility and political memory. It may put armies already in the threatened region on a defensive order, or note or dismiss the letter. A warning never declares war, creates or ends an agreement, or forces acceptance. A human recipient receives a bilingual log entry and keeps the decision. The sighted court never learns that it was reported, and the sender does not learn the recipient's decision. No warning, and no sighting, is never evidence of an undefended frontier.

In the ordinary openings no court meets the friendship threshold, so no warnings are sent. `node scripts/multipolar-sim.mjs` adds a fictional allied-frontier opening (Armenia and the Senate friendly, the Senate already at war with a prepared Parthia) in which warnings, delivery and decisions are unforced. Existing saves initialize the queue lazily.
## Warning Chronicle privacy

Scout warnings have no return courier. Their sender therefore receives no Chronicle receipt when the message arrives and no notification of the recipient's decision, loss, delay or expiry. The existing outbound ledger remains dispatch-only. A human recipient still learns the dated warning on actual arrival and decides independently; no troops are ordered automatically. `test/warning-chronicle-privacy.test.js` checks real seeded delivery/loss/delay, bilingual received notices and save migration in addition to the ledger projection tests.

Recipient gratitude from an unacknowledged scout warning remains in that recipient's private political memory. It does not immediately alter the shared bilateral relation score, which the sender could otherwise inspect as a delivery/decision signal. The privacy regression also checks the relation value while preserving the recipient's helpful-warning memory.

## Consented joint operations

The existing Operations screen in both map views lets a player reserve an approved, stationary offensive plan and pay 5 treasury to invite a full, offensive or defensive treaty partner reported at war with the same enemy. An incoming invitation can be accepted by reserving a separately approved own plan for 2 treasury, declined, or left unanswered. These fees cover subsequent correspondence; ordinary preparation and army upkeep continue. No country gains command of another's army and no war or treaty is created.

Invitation, reply and final confirmation all use the existing mailbox, postal addresses, month-based travel and courier losses. The proposed departure month allows three estimated journeys and a reply window. A recipient releases orders only after the final confirmation arrives before that month. A sender that received an acceptance and posted its confirmation may therefore act alone if the confirmation is lost or late. The sender never inspects the partner's live plan, receipt, later instructions or private decision. The agreed month releases automatic orders or assisted recommendations; it is not proof that either side moved or won a battle.

Each plan stores only its own reservation and immutable correspondence references. Government replacement, a different own war generation, new own wars, changed own orders, lost forces, a newly reported controller or a broken own partnership can invalidate that court's reservation. Withdrawal notices must travel too. A missed or invalid reservation pauses its own plan; a player can explicitly withdraw and resume independently. Separate fronts can be coordinated, but same-theater forces still use the existing independent movement and battle rules. There is no shared battle army or guaranteed arrival synchronization.

AI courts can initiate invitations and independently accept, decline or build their own prepared plan using their own resources, idle troops, reports, treaty relations and political memories. Planning is bounded to one outstanding commitment and a twelve-month invitation cooldown per court. A reserved plan prevents ordinary AI orders, support mergers and routine reorganization from taking its armies away. Other idle troops do not attack or route through that court's reserved objectives before the agreed month; defense and other objectives remain independent. A third party can still capture an objective and invalidate the reservation. Advanced cooperation is not introduced automatically at beginner difficulty. Declining leaves the recipient's ordinary plans independent; hidden refusal, transport failure and private changes do not become sender knowledge.

`test/joint-operations.test.js` covers fees, three journeys, real same-month orders, final-confirmation loss and lateness, refusal/silence, hidden-plan invariance, changed wars/courts/reports, explicit independent resumption, assisted mode and save migration. Its two 24-seed four-court scenarios (four active fronts and initially neutral Egypt) run Rome, Egypt, Parthia and Armenia under AI control; starting alliances, wars and resources are scenario premises, while messages and decisions remain unforced. `tools/joint-operations-smoke.mjs` uses actual controls in both views for invitation, acceptance, confirmation, save/load and the real next-month departure, and checks refusal and silence in the campaign view. Cloud results must be reviewed before merging; this feature does not complete the remaining territorial bargaining, mediation, long-run balance or map-performance work.

Both map entry points use the existing shared campaign Operations screen; returning to the terrain atlas carries the same campaign and orders. The browser check follows that real navigation rather than trying to click hidden controls from the departed page. Cooperation memories are private to the court receiving the relevant answer, confirmation or withdrawal; there is no shared relation-score receipt. The AI scenario also records actual moving armies and requires at least one independently commanded pair to depart in the same agreed month.

## Received treaty proposals and hidden sender changes

For limited treaty types, including non-aggression, an AI recipient evaluates the posted proposal rather than the sender's live treasury, current territorial survival or subsequently changed policy. New letters carry diplomatic quality and stated credibility as dispatch-time credentials; changing the sender's hidden state during transit does not rewrite the paper. The recipient still uses its own current relation, political memories, war and existing agreement. Missing credentials in old pending letters give no diplomacy bonus and a neutral reliability baseline, without reconstructing foreign private policy.

The sender must still possess territory and pay the actual treaty fee when the acceptance returns. Spending or recovering funds while couriers travel can therefore prevent or permit conclusion, without changing what the recipient decided earlier. Existing four-month limited-treaty correspondence, human-consent confirmations, fees, refusal and loss remain in place. Full-alliance recipient review likewise uses its own current war and alliance obligations, its own surviving meeting participants and dated delivered reports of the sender's wars or allies, rather than the remote court's live state. Stale reports can produce a mistaken refusal; future-dated reports are ignored. Final conclusion still applies actual legal compatibility. Named-war neutrality is described below. `test/agreement-recipient-knowledge.test.js` checks hidden-state invariance for every limited treaty type, delayed real payment, recovery, dated credentials, own changed relations/war and old-save handling. `test/alliance-recipient-knowledge.test.js` checks hidden foreign wars, alliances, lost territory, reported conflicts, future reports and contact-credit ownership.

## Named-war neutrality

The Government → Diplomatic agreements form, reached from either map, now offers a twelve-month neutrality commitment with an explicit current enemy selector. The requester pays the existing 2 treasury courier fee, then 6 treasury only on conclusion. The partner promises not to initiate war against either named belligerent, provide new direct military funds/food, or grant them new military access. The two signatories also promise not to attack each other. Trade, unrelated fronts and independent self-defense remain possible; the treaty does not end wars, recall existing orders or confiscate supplies already dispatched. The fixed twelve-month term starts on conclusion and continues if the original war ends. A unilateral breach uses the existing 20-point credibility and bilateral-relation penalty and requires confirmation in the UI.

The existing proposal ledger and correspondence handle offer, refusal, silence, return loss and human final confirmation. The outgoing war identity is bound at dispatch, so an ended/restarted conflict cannot ratify old terms. Recipients evaluate their own belligerency and military obligations from the received proposal; sending does not inspect their secret obligations. The requester rechecks actual signing conditions on conclusion. No army is created, transferred or directly commanded by the agreement.

War declarations, automatic military obligations, direct aid decisions and scheduled military-support payments enforce the promise. A neutral human can decline aid or explicitly end its own pledge before answering; a requester never learns a secret refusal reason. The Government terms, both-map war controls, power-network labels, mailbox and searchable atlas help expose the mechanic in Chinese and English. No neutrality record is invented in old saves.

Hard/expert courts may independently propose named-war neutrality in the existing annual frontier review instead of non-aggression. They use own wars, contacts and dated reports, with a bounded random choice; partners can decline. The reproducible test varies cautious trade relations and close friendship across 24 seeds, starts a fictional Egyptian–Parthian war, and runs Rome, Egypt, Parthia and Armenia both under full AI control and with a passive Roman player. Only initial conditions are set; proposals, answers and treaties are unforced.

`test/neutrality.test.js` checks real fees and delays, terms, war and aid restrictions, unrelated aid/trade, self-defense, automatic guarantees, actual breaches, expiry, old saves, losses, stale wars, human confirmation and autonomous proposals. `tools/all-agreement-types-smoke.mjs` includes neutrality as a real outgoing player flow. `tools/neutrality-smoke.mjs` follows real incoming decisions, confirmation, saving/loading, both-map restrictions, bilingual terms, help, refusal of prohibited aid and explicit breach before dispatching resources. Require reviewed cloud results before merging. This does not complete general territorial bargaining, treaty-observation privacy or terrain performance.

## Preparing and evaluating coalition invitations

The Government form now chooses two distinct contacted courts at peace with the initiating court and a separate known threat. Contact is determined from that court's own frontier or diplomatic records, ignoring future-dated reports. Selecting a different member refreshes the alternatives. Missing contacts, insufficient own funds, own conflicting obligations, pending invitations and the twelve-month repeat interval are explained before dispatch. Previewing does not inspect foreign treasuries, opinions, territorial survival or undisclosed treaties. Existing reports may be stale; correspondence resolves the uncertainty.

New invitations carry dispatch-time credibility. AI recipients use that statement and their own relations, resources, perceived threat and military obligations, rather than changes to the sender's private credibility during travel. Old letters without credentials use the neutral baseline. A received acceptance remains consent: final formation rechecks actual resources and legal compatibility, but does not silently reevaluate a recipient's opinions using new remote state. An AI initiator may still reconsider from its own current threat assessment. Human recipients can refuse or leave an invitation unanswered; their own conflicting wars, alliances, non-aggression or neutrality obligations prevent an invalid acceptance without charging fees or consuming the reply.

The existing three-treasury invitation, five-treasury membership, travel, refusal, silence, losses and separate human confirmation remain. Formation does not declare war or give command of another member's troops. The form and own-conflict feedback are bilingual and accessible through the atlas's existing Government handoff. `test/coalition-preparation.test.js` checks own-state preview, posted credentials, old saves, sealed consent and real settlement constraints; `tools/coalition-courier-smoke.mjs` checks defaults, edits, paid dispatch, saved pending letters, explanations and both-view access. Cloud results must be reviewed before release. This does not resolve the broader own-treaty observation/privacy limitation described in the development audit.
