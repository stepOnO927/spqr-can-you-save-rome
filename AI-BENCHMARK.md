# AI benchmark

Run `node scripts/benchmark-ai.mjs > benchmark-results.json` for a passive opponent. In PowerShell, run `$env:SPQR_AUTOPLAY='1'; node scripts/benchmark-ai.mjs > benchmark-active-results.json` for automated opposing orders. The latter holds that opponent's military AI at Standard while the measured side varies. Each checked-in result covers 20 paired seeds, five scenarios, both Roman sides, three measured difficulties and 24 months; the optional economic handicap is off. Different decisions may consume random numbers in different orders even with paired starting seeds.

The JSON reports means per run: reserve/stability/solvency objectives met (out of three), battle losses, months with food below 20, months with negative treasury, active military commitments, theaters lost, attacks whose retrospective battle power was below 80% of the defender, total attacks, ending stability, crisis months and transitions out of crisis. Battle power is used only for retrospective evaluation; AI decisions use its own sightings and estimates. Lost theaters are a proxy for breakthroughs, not proof of a successful response.

To exercise coalition obligations, the three-state scenario starts with the same additional scripted Senate declaration of war on Armenia in every run. `coalitionJoined` counts members who actually entered that war, `coalitionBattles` counts their battles against the target, `coalitionRetained` counts their original theaters still held by coalition members at the end (three at the start), `coalitionFunding` counts actual treasury transfers to the leader, and `coalitionSupplies` counts food transferred from members' stocks. These measures are zero outside that scenario.

Against a passive opponent in the 49 BCE campaign, the Senate on Standard/Hard/Grandmaster averaged 8,260/5,144/5,144 casualties and 2/1/1 unfavorable attacks. Against automated Standard orders, it averaged 8,849/8,112/4,271 casualties, 0.75/0/0 unfavorable attacks, and 6.10/4.30/1.80 theaters lost. In the automated-opponent coalition runs measured from the Senate side, all three members joined, transferred 0 treasury and 14 food on average, fought 3/3/3 battles, and retained 3.00/1.15/1.00 of their original three theaters. In the passive-opponent revolution scenario, Caesar on Standard remained in crisis for all 24 months with no recovery, while the harder settings recovered once. Full results for every scenario and side are in the two JSON files.

These are not general rankings or win-rate claims. Automated orders are a controlled test mode, not a human player; the designated player still receives player-specific political treatment, and turn order differs between sides. The coalition attack is injected rather than naturally negotiated. The short campaigns, mostly stable starting economies and limited scenario-specific events leave long-term recovery and political outcomes insufficiently sampled. Several paired outcomes are identical, and Caesar in the revolution scenario still loses theaters under stronger settings. More varied active opponents, deliberate economic shocks and longer campaigns are needed for robust balancing.

`node scripts/performance-ai.mjs` measured 120 Grandmaster months in the three-state setup on the development machine: 16.74 ms mean, 44.13 ms at the 95th percentile and 102.14 ms maximum per simulation tick, ending with 36 characters and 24 theaters. This measures Node simulation time, not browser rendering or performance on slower devices.
